How do we study intelligence in an animal that cannot tell us what it is thinking?

Lemur cognition offers a way to explore that question—and to reconsider what a test of intelligence actually measures.

Start with a question

Intelligence is more than one score.

Researchers investigate different abilities separately. Duke’s lemur cognition research includes comparative tests of inhibitory control, social skills, and spatial memory. These tasks help explore how cognition evolved across species. [1]

01

Remembering locations

Spatial-memory tasks investigate whether an animal can remember where something was located. The challenge is distinguishing memory from cues such as smell.

02

Responding to others

Social-cognition tasks explore how animals respond to information about other individuals. Success in one social task does not establish human-like understanding.

03

Resisting an impulse

Inhibitory-control tasks examine whether an animal can suppress an immediate response. Motivation and task design also matter when interpreting performance.

These are explanations of research concepts, not instructions for experimenting with animals.

Reading the evidence

Two studies. A richer picture.

Social groups and social cognition

A 2013 study reported that group size predicted performance in the social tasks examined, but not the nonsocial tasks. The finding supports a relationship between social environment and particular cognitive abilities, rather than a universal ranking of species. [2]

Cognitive control and ecology

A 2022 study tested 39 lemurs using a battery of cognitive-control tasks. Ecological complexity was a more consistent predictor than social complexity across the contexts examined. Different tasks and hypotheses can reveal different aspects of cognition. [3]

Questions across disciplines

What might this teach us about AI?

A useful question is whether performing well on a task means a system has learned the ability we intended to test. An animal, a human, or an AI model might succeed by using an unexpected shortcut.

This is a conceptual connection, not evidence that lemurs and AI learn in the same way. Comparing them requires careful definitions and methods.

Questions to keep asking

  • Could the subject solve the task using an unintended cue?
  • Does learning transfer to a new situation?
  • How do sensory abilities affect the fairness of a test?
  • Are we measuring understanding, memory, motivation—or a mixture?
Research and responsibility

The Duke Lemur Center

The Duke Lemur Center combines non-invasive research with conservation and education. Its research includes observational studies of behaviour as well as investigations of cognition and biomechanics. [4]

Understanding animal minds also means paying attention to animal welfare and the environments in which animals live.
